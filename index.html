I am a comparative political scientist studying legal institutions, violence, and the political economy of development, with a regional focus on South Asia. Currently, I am an ETH Postdoctoral Fellow at the International Relations and Data Science (IRDS) Lab in the Department of Humanities, Social and Political Sciences (D-GESS) at ETH Zurich. I received my PhD in Political Science from the University of California, Berkeley, and hold an MPA/ID from the Harvard Kennedy School. I am also a Research Associate at the Center on the Politics of Development.

My research examines how individuals make claims on legal and kinship institutions under conditions of inequality and dependence. My current work focuses on access to justice in cases of domestic violence and land dispossession in India. A second strand of my research examines immigration and carceral institutions in the United States.

Across projects, I combine intensive fieldwork with field and online experiments, original surveys, administrative and archival data, and text-as-data methods.

Before my PhD, I worked as a development economist in India with organizations including J-PAL, IDinsight, and the World Bank, as well as with government agencies and grassroots organizations.
